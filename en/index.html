Dissertation

PhD dissertation (122 Computer Science): “Optimization of Parameters and Structure of Neural Networks Using Evolutionary Algorithms”. Defended on 28 August 2026 before one-time specialized academic council DF 26.002.419 at Igor Sikorsky KPI; unanimous vote.

TESA-26

Weight pruning. An evolutionary search (CMA-ES) decides what share of weights each layer keeps. Best Friedman rank (2.04) among 5 methods on 6 datasets; significantly better than SparseGPT, WANDA and RIA.

GFCS

Converts a sparse network into a compact dense one without training data or retraining. Compresses the model 7.6× and preserves quality on all 8 datasets studied.

SACMA-DAC and SACMA-MAB

Hyperparameter search. CMA-ES explores candidates on a surrogate model (Random Forest), and only one candidate per iteration gets a full training run. Ranked 1st and 2nd among 9 methods on 43 tasks, with the fastest convergence.

ENT

A genetic algorithm merges two networks trained on different classes into a single model. On MNIST the merged model recognizes all 10 classes; accuracy after calibration is 0.917.

Applicability limit

Showed that evolutionary algorithms work well for low-dimensional structural decisions, while gradient methods remain better at training network weights.

Publications

1 Scopus-indexed paper, 3 papers in Ukrainian peer-reviewed journals, 1 conference abstract.

  1. Effectiveness of evolutionary algorithms in neural-network optimization (2026)

    Compared evolutionary (CMA-ES), gradient-based and Bayesian methods on unconstrained minimization, structured pruning and hyperparameter optimization. CMA-ES + SynFlow with energy compensation gave the best quality under extreme pruning (98%, F1 = 0.81). Reported the limits honestly: Adam trains weights better, and magnitude pruning wins at moderate sparsity.

    Hirianskyi, B., & Bulakh, B. (2026). Effectiveness of evolutionary algorithms in neural network optimization tasks: Unconditional minimization, pruning, and hyperparameter optimization. Control, Navigation and Communication Systems, 1(83), 45–50. https://doi.org/10.26906/sunz.2026.1.045

  2. Neuroevolutionary synthesis of neural networks (2025, in Ukrainian)

    Implemented two neuroevolution algorithms from scratch, memetic and hybrid, that automatically select network architecture and weights. On non-linear binary-classification tasks (XOR, Moons, Breast Cancer) they outperformed a fixed-architecture perceptron. Python, NumPy, scikit-learn.

    Hirianskyi, B., & Bulakh, B. (2025). [Creating neural networks through neuroevolution and automated learning using evolutionary algorithms]. Science and Technology Today, 8(49), 1214–1227. https://doi.org/10.52058/2786-6025-2025-8(49)-1214-1227

  3. Evolutionary optimization of neural networks (2024)

    Applied genetic algorithms (DEAP, sklearn-genetic) to optimize the architecture, hyperparameters and weights of a multilayer perceptron on the Wine Quality dataset. Reported honestly that the evolutionary approach did not, on average, beat the baseline in quality and required far more computation.

    Hirianskyi, B., & Bulakh, B. (2024). Using evolutionary algorithms for training and optimizing neural networks. Science and Technology Today, 8(36), 808–823. https://doi.org/10.52058/2786-6025-2024-8(36)-808-823

  4. A review of evolutionary algorithms for neural-network synthesis and training (2023, Scopus-indexed)

    Systematized how genetic algorithms are applied to neural networks: feature selection, weight optimization, changes to connection structure, and optimizer tuning.

    Hirianskyi, B., & Bulakh, B. (2023). A review of practice of using evolutionary algorithms for neural network synthesis and training. Technology Audit and Production Reserves, 4(2(72)), 22–26. https://doi.org/10.15587/2706-5448.2023.286278
